From First-Year Foundations to Third-Year Gratitude

As we sit here in our third-year BSCS classes, surrounded by lines of code and algorithms that feel like second nature now, it is easy to forget the shaky ground we stood on during that very first advisory session. It was the start of college, a whirlwind of new faces, unfamiliar syllabi, and the daunting promise of Mathematics that loomed like an unsolved equation. That is when Sir Alhamid entered our lives, not just as our first adviser, but as the steady hand that would guide us through the initial turbulence of higher education. I remember the way he would break down complex theorems in Math in the Modern World and pre-Calculus into something almost conversational, like sharing a secret over coffee rather than lecturing from a pedestal. Math, which had always felt like a cold, abstract beast to many of us, transformed under his guidance. He would pause mid-lesson to draw a quick sketch on the board, connecting derivatives to real-world curves or integrals to the flow of everyday decisions, making us see the beauty in the logic. It was not rote memorization; it was discovery. His passion was infectious, turning “what if” questions into “aha” moments that built our confidence brick by brick. Even now, when we are debugging programs or optimizing data structures, I catch echoes of those early lessons, his emphasis on precision not as a chore, but as a pathway to clarity and creativity. But what lingers deepest is not just the math; it is the man behind the chalk. Sir Alhamid’s kindness was the quiet force that made his classroom a sanctuary. In a sea of first-year anxieties, missed deadlines, imposter syndrome, the fear of not belonging, he was there with an open door and an even more open heart. He would listen without interruption, offering advice wrapped in encouragement rather than criticism. “You are capable of more than you know,” he would say, his eyes crinkling with genuine belief, and somehow, you would start to believe it too. His gratitude, too, was a gift we did not expect. At the end of every session, he would thank us for our questions, our energy, our willingness to try. It flipped the script; we were not just students receiving knowledge, but partners in a shared journey. That humility made him approachable, human, and utterly irreplaceable. Looking back from this vantage point, three years in, it is clear how foundational he was. Our former advisory class was not just a group; it was the root system that nourished everything that followed. The resilience we built in those Math problems mirrors the grit we need for late-night coding marathons. His kindness taught us empathy, a vital soft skill in team projects and collaborative tech worlds. And his gratitude? It is a reminder to pause amid our busy schedules and appreciate the mentors who shaped us. We have grown from wide-eyed freshmen to third-year trailblazers, but Sir Alhamid remains the constant, the first thread in the tapestry of our college story. On this Teachers’ Day, as we reflect with full hearts, we want him to know: Thank you, Sir Alhamid, for being more than an adviser. You did not just teach us equations; you showed us how to solve for ourselves. Your impact is not measured in grades or credits, but in the quiet confidence we carry forward. We are grateful, we are kinder because of you, and above all, we hold a deep, enduring love for the teacher who made our beginnings feel like possibilities. Here is to you, the one who turned our first steps into a lifelong stride.
